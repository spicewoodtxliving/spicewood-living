Keeping a Spicewood home running

Living outside city limits means more of your home's systems are up to you. Here's how utilities work in Spicewood, plus local pros for the jobs that come up.

Set up your utilities

Electric

Usually PEC

Much of Spicewood is served by Pedernales Electric Cooperative, a member-owned co-op. Confirm the provider for your address before closing.

Water

Depends on the street

Some neighborhoods have a public supply, utility district or village system (Briarcliff runs its own). Many acreage homes use a private well. Ask which one, and for a recent water test on a well.

Sewer

Mostly septic

Septic systems are permitted by the county. Ask the seller for the permit and the last pumping record. Burnet County septic info · State homeowner guide

Internet

Check before you buy

Fiber reaches some subdivisions, not all. Cable, fixed wireless and satellite fill the gaps. Look the exact address up with providers before you fall in love with a house.

Gas

Often propane

Rural homes without a gas line use a propane tank for heat, water heaters or cooking. Find out if the tank is owned or leased, and from which company.

Trash

Private haulers

There's no city pickup in unincorporated areas. Neighborhoods often share a hauler; ask neighbors or your POA who services the street.
